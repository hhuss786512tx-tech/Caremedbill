What Is a Clean Claim Rate and Why Should Physicians Care?

Care Med Billing team Core Billing & RCM

Clean claim rate measures the percentage of claims that are accepted and paid by a payer the first time they're submitted: no errors, no missing information, no resubmission required. It's one of the clearest single indicators of how well a practice's billing process is actually working.

Why This Metric Matters So Much

Every claim that isn't clean has to be identified, corrected, and resubmitted, which costs staff time and delays payment. A low clean claim rate doesn't just mean lost revenue; it means a meaningful share of staff hours are spent reworking claims that should have gone out correctly the first time.

What Counts as a "Clean" Claim

A clean claim has accurate patient demographics, active and correctly matched insurance information, valid procedure and diagnosis codes, any required modifiers, and documentation that supports medical necessity, all present and correct before it's ever submitted.

What's a Good Clean Claim Rate?

Industry benchmarks generally put a strong clean claim rate in the mid-to-high 90s. Practices operating meaningfully below that, particularly in the 70s or 80s, are typically losing significant staff time and cash flow to avoidable rework.

How to Improve It

  • Verify eligibility and demographics before every single visit
  • Use claim scrubbing software before submission, not after denial
  • Standardize documentation templates around medical necessity
  • Track denial reasons and fix the root process, not just the individual claim

Clean claim rate is one of the first numbers we review during a free practice audit, because it tends to reveal exactly where a billing process is breaking down.
