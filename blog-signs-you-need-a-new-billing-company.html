Signs Your Practice May Need a New Medical Billing Company

Care Med Billing Editorial Team Core Billing & RCM

Switching billing partners is disruptive, so most practices put it off far longer than they should — often absorbing real revenue loss in the process. These are the warning signs worth taking seriously.

Your Reports Don't Answer Basic Questions

If you can't easily find your clean claim rate, days in accounts receivable, or denial trends without asking and waiting, your billing partner isn't giving you the visibility needed to manage the practice's finances.

Denials Keep Repeating

One denial is normal. The same denial reason showing up month after month means the root cause is never actually being fixed — just individually resubmitted each time it recurs.

Aging Accounts Receivable Keeps Growing

A steadily increasing balance in the 90+ day A/R bucket, without a clear explanation, usually means claims aren't being worked on a consistent schedule.

Communication Is Slow or One-Directional

You should be able to ask a billing question and get a substantive answer within a business day or two. Long silences, especially around denied or delayed claims, are a red flag.

No Specialty-Specific Expertise

General billing knowledge isn't enough for specialties with complex coding rules — cardiology, surgery, oncology, and others each have denial patterns a generalist team may not recognize.

What to Look for Instead

A strong billing partner provides transparent, real-time reporting, proactively flags denial trends before they become chronic, and has coders with specific experience in your specialty — not just general medical billing familiarity.
