What Is Remote Patient Monitoring (RPM)?

Care Med Billing Editorial Team RPM & CCM

Remote Patient Monitoring, or RPM, uses connected devices — blood pressure cuffs, glucose meters, pulse oximeters, and similar tools — to collect a patient's health data at home and transmit it to their care team. It's become one of the fastest-growing reimbursable programs in medicine, particularly for practices managing chronic disease.

How RPM Actually Works

A patient is enrolled in an RPM program, given (or already owns) a connected monitoring device, and takes readings on a regular basis. That data is transmitted automatically to the practice, where clinical staff review it and respond to any concerning trends before they become an emergency.

Which Patients Benefit Most

RPM is most valuable for patients managing chronic conditions that benefit from ongoing trend data rather than a single in-office snapshot — hypertension, diabetes, COPD, heart failure, and similar conditions are the most common use cases.

Why Practices Are Adding RPM

  • It creates a new, recurring monthly revenue stream tied to time already spent reviewing patient data
  • It improves clinical outcomes by catching concerning trends earlier
  • It strengthens the ongoing relationship between patient and practice between visits
  • It's billable separately from standard office visit E/M codes

Getting Started

The biggest barrier to RPM adoption usually isn't clinical — it's billing and documentation. Programs have to be enrolled, devices tracked, time logged, and claims submitted correctly to actually get reimbursed for the work being done. A billing partner experienced in RPM removes that barrier.
